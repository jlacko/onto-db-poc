# Competency questions: the specification

This file is the spec of the Kriminalita ontology, and it is executable.
`scripts/validate.py` reads every ` ```expected ` block below and compares it
with the result of the matching SPARQL query (`ontology/tests/cq/CQnn.rq`, run on
the sample RDF) and SQL query (`CQnn.sql`, run on a SQLite database generated
from the same sample). So each question is answered three ways: **by you, by
the ontology, by the database**. A disagreement points to a gap in the
ontology, a bug in a query, or a question that was not precise enough.

## How to work with this file

1. Open the sample data in a spreadsheet: [`tests/sample-records.csv`](tests/sample-records.csv)
   (13 records, each with its area, municipality, ORP area, okres and kraj),
   [`tests/sample-types.csv`](tests/sample-types.csv) (the offence
   types used, with broader type and category) and
   [`tests/sample-areas.csv`](tests/sample-areas.csv) (the anonymisation areas, with
   municipality and centre). All three are generated from
   `tests/sample-data.ttl`, so don't edit them. UTF-8, comma-separated, dot
   decimals; a record's types and categories are separated by spaces.
2. For each question marked **✍ your answer**, work the answer out by hand
   from the sample table and replace `TODO` in its `expected` block.
3. Read the *Decision* line. If you disagree with the proposed modelling
   decision, rewrite it. The decision is part of the spec, and the queries must
   follow it.
4. Run `.venv/bin/python scripts/validate.py`. Your answer either passes, or
   it shows you where you, the ontology or the database disagree.

Format of an `expected` block: a header row, then one row per result, columns
separated by `|`. Row order does not matter. Decimals are compared to 3 places.
`TODO` anywhere in the block marks the question as pending. A block may start
with `rows: N; others: 0`: the result then has exactly N rows, and every row you
do not write out is 0 (see *territorial units* below).

Terms: a *record* is one offence record, one per occurrence; a *category* is a
top-level offence type (no broader type). Time means **report time**
(`krim:reportDateTime`, local date as published), and periods (a month, a date
range, a year) are parameters of a question, not stored concepts. An
*anonymisation area* is one of the publisher's predefined Voronoi polygons; a
record's published point is its area's centre. A *municipality* is an *obec*
of ČSÚ codelist 43; every area lies in exactly one (see `concepts.md`, S6–S7). A
municipality lies in exactly one *ORP* (správní obvod obce s rozšířenou
působností, ČSÚ codelist 65) and one *okres* (LAU 1, codelist 101); each ORP and
each okres lies in exactly one *kraj* (NUTS 3, codelist 100). The two branches
meet at the kraj (S8–S12). The Czech terms are used as they are: "district" and
"region" are ambiguous in English. The
publisher's monthly download files are pagination and appear in no question.

**Territorial units in breakdowns.** A breakdown by anonymisation area,
municipality, ORP, okres or kraj lists **every unit in scope, with 0 where
nothing was reported**: a unit exists whether or not anything happened in it.
The scope is all units of that level in the ČSÚ codelist (a nationwide
question), or all units inside a named parent (the areas of one municipality,
the okresy of one kraj). The number of units in scope is a codebook fact; the
counting is the decision. A hand answer writes only the non-zero rows and
starts with `rows: N; others: 0`. One exception: a rate needs a denominator, so
a unit with no remaining records has no rate and is omitted (CQ25).

## A. Counting and classification

### CQ01: Records per month of report
**Question.** How many offence records were reported in each calendar month?
**Decision.** Counted by the local date of `krim:reportDateTime`; the month is

**Question.** How many records reported between 2025-02-01 and 2025-02-28

### CQ03: Clearance rate per category ✍ your answer
**Question.** For each category present in the sample, how many records are
there, how many have a probable offender identified (clearance state 1), and
what is the clearance rate?
**Decision (proposed, please review).** Records concluded as "act did not
occur" (state 3) or "act is not an offence" (state 4) are **excluded from the
denominator**: they are not offences that could be cleared. Rate =
cleared / remaining records. Categories with no remaining records (all of
their records excluded as state 3 or 4) are **omitted**: with nothing to
clear they have no rate, and a rate of 0 would misread as a failure to clear.
The rate is a decimal between 0 and 1, **compared to 3 decimal places**
(write 0.667, not 0.67).

### CQ06: Misdemeanours versus the rest ✍ your answer
**Question.** For each calendar month of report, how many records are
misdemeanours only, how many are not misdemeanours, and how many are both?
**Decision.** Crime and misdemeanour are **not exclusive**: one record may be
both (domain expert's decision). A record "is a misdemeanour" when at least one
of its categories is *Misdemeanours* (code 97), and "is other" when at least one
of its categories is not 97. Each record falls into exactly one group:
*misdemeanours* (only 97), *other* (no 97), *both* (97 and another category).
The three groups add up to the month's total (CQ01). "Other" includes crimes
but also fires and traffic accidents (open question 4).
**Check.** The three numbers in each row add up to the month's total in CQ01;
decide by a record's *categories*, not its types (types such as 76 or 111 are
subgroups of 97).
**Your notes on the decision:** _…_

```expected
month | misdemeanours | other | both
2025-01 | 1 | 4 | 0 
2025-02 | 2 | 5 | 0
2025-03 | 0 | 0 | 1
```

### CQ12: Most frequent most-specific types ✍ your answer
**Question.** Among records reported in February 2025, how many carry each
*most-specific* offence type?
**Decision.** A type is most-specific for a record when the record carries no
narrower type of it. (Records carry their types together with all broader
types, so e.g. `18, 19` means "Burglary – flat"; only 19 counts here.)

```expected
type | records
42 |  1
19 |  2
111 | 1
102 | 1
54 | 1
113 | 1

```

### CQ13: Records spanning two categories ✍ your answer
**Question.** Which records combine offence types from two or more different
categories, and which categories?
**Decision.** Categories are the top-level ancestors of a record's types (the
`categories` column of `sample-records.csv`). Several types in the same category
count once: `18 19` is one category. List only records with two or more
categories, and write **one row per record and category**: a record with two
categories gives two rows, a record with one category is not listed. Example:
a record 9999 with categories `18 35` gives the rows `9999 | 18` and `9999 | 35`.

```expected
record | category
1004 | 1
1004 | 84
1013 | 63
1013 | 97
```

### CQ11: As of when
**Question.** As of when is the state of record 1003 known, and as of when is
the whole dataset?
**Decision.** Only the latest observed state of a record is kept. A record's
`krim:stateAsOf` is when the publisher reported that state; the dataset is as of
the latest such moment. In the database the loader also records the time of the
latest load run (technical table `load_run`).

```expected

2026-11-19T00:25:02 | 2026-11-19T00:27:18
```

## D. Identity and continuity of the history

### CQ18: No duplicates
**Question.** Is any occurrence recorded more than once (the same record
identifier on two records)?
**Decision.** One occurrence is one record, whichever download file and refresh
it came from. When the publisher reports a record again (e.g. first as unsolved,
later as cleared), the later report *replaces* the earlier one. The expected
answer is an empty result. The loader test (`validate.py`, section 8) feeds the
same record in two observations, in both orders, and expects one row in the
later state.

```expected
record | occurrences
```

### CQ19: Holes in the history
**Question.** Which days between 2025-01-01 and 2025-01-07 have no reported
records?
**Decision.** SQL only (SPARQL cannot generate a calendar). On real data a
missing day points to a download that failed, not to a crime-free day; the
loader runs the same check over the whole year.

```expected
day
2025-01-01
2025-01-02
2025-01-03
2025-01-04
2025-01-06
2025-01-07
```

### CQ23: Records outside every area
**Question.** Is there a record that does not lie in a known anonymisation
area?
**Decision.** Every record lies in exactly one area (`concepts.md`, S6). In
the model a record holds its area, not a point, so here the question checks
that the area exists. Matching the publisher's point to an area's centre is
the loader's job: a point that is no area's centre is a data error, or a sign
that the publisher has changed the areas; it is reported, never placed in the
nearest area. The expected answer is an empty result.

```expected
record
```

### CQ28: The two branches agree
**Question.** Is there a municipality whose kraj differs depending on whether
it is reached through the ORP or through the okres?
**Decision.** ORP and okres are two branches that meet at the kraj (S11).
A municipality's kraj must be the same either way; this is a hard rule. The
expected answer is an empty result. Answered from reference data only.

```expected
municipality
```

### CQ29: ORPs spanning several okresy
**Question.** Which ORPs have municipalities in more than one okres, and in
how many?
**Decision.** An ORP normally lies within one okres (S12), but this is a
soft rule: a warning, not a constraint. The known exception is ORP Turnov
(5109), whose municipalities lie in three okresy. Answered from reference
data only. A new row here means ČSÚ has changed the codelists.

```expected
orp | okresy
5109 | 3
```
